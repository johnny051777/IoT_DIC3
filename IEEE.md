# GridReserve Agent: Weather-Driven Load Forecasting and Unit-Scenario Reasoning for Taiwan Power System Reserve Risk

## Abstract

This study proposes **GridReserve Agent**, a domain-specific artificial intelligence (AI) agent for reserve-risk assessment in the Taiwan power system. Unlike conventional LLM-based grid agents that primarily focus on power-flow analysis, contingency screening, and simulated network models, the proposed system integrates public operational data, weather observations, unit characteristics, and domain knowledge to support load forecasting, unit-outage scenario reasoning, low-reserve attribution, and supply-light interpretation.

The system separates the problem into two components: load forecasting, which is primarily driven by calendar and meteorological features, and reserve-risk reasoning, which depends on dispatch outcomes, unit availability, and outage scenarios. A tool-constrained architecture ensures that all numerical results are generated by deterministic modules and verified before being presented to users. The expected contributions include a reproducible Taiwan power-grid knowledge base, a data-quality correction workflow, a quantifiable evaluation of LLM hallucination and traceability, and a decision-oriented reserve-risk analysis framework.

**Index Terms**—Reserve margin, load forecasting, scenario analysis, large language model (LLM), AI agent, power-system reliability, Taiwan power system.

## I. Introduction

Taiwan is facing a simultaneous increase in electricity demand and supply-side uncertainty. Taipower forecasts that the 2026 summer peak load will reach 42.94 GW, while the nighttime peak will reach 38.53 GW, both record highs. The projected increase from the 2025 peak of 40.92 GW is approximately 4.89%, mainly driven by semiconductor manufacturing and AI-related electricity demand. [1]

The supply side is also vulnerable to unexpected outages. In September 2025, an explosion occurred during the commissioning test of Hsinta Unit 2, while Linkou Unit 2 also experienced a failure. Taipower reported a combined reduction of approximately 2.1 GW in available capacity, equivalent to approximately 6% of the nighttime peak. [1]

These events indicate that the reserve margin is not a static reliability indicator. Instead, it is a dynamic operational outcome affected by unit failures, fuel constraints, renewable-generation variability, and dispatch decisions. Therefore, a system that can explain reserve risk, simulate unit-outage scenarios, and trace every numerical result is needed.

## II. Problem Statement

Although Taiwan provides substantial public power-system data, existing tools do not adequately support non-expert users in understanding whether the system is secure, how a unit outage would affect the reserve margin, and why a particular supply-light level occurs.

First, the reserve margin is a dispatch result rather than a purely weather-driven variable. Preliminary analysis of 1,339 days of data shows that the correlation between peak load and the average maximum temperature across three weather stations is approximately 0.75, whereas the correlation between the reserve margin and temperature is only approximately -0.07. This indicates that reserve margin cannot be directly predicted from weather or time-series features alone. [1]

Second, the quantity of reserve does not necessarily represent its quality. During the 2021 May 13 and May 17 outages, the reserve margin was approximately 10%, but fast-ramping units accounted for only about 2% of the reserve. Other units required several hours to ramp up, while coal-unit restarts, depleted pumped-storage resources, and declining solar generation further reduced the actually dispatchable capacity. [1]

Third, existing LLM-based grid agents mainly target simulated networks, optimal power flow, N-1 contingency analysis, and violation repair. They do not directly address Taiwan’s public operational data, reserve-margin rules, and unit-outage scenarios. Moreover, LLM hallucination remains a major concern in safety-critical power-system applications. [1]

## III. Proposed Approach

GridReserve Agent is designed as a domain-specific AI agent that supports reserve-risk assessment using Taiwan’s public operational data. Rather than directly predicting the reserve margin, the system decomposes the problem into load forecasting and supply-side scenario reasoning.

For load forecasting, the system uses calendar, holiday, trend, and meteorological features from Taipei, Taichung, and Kaohsiung. The model outputs the 10th, 50th, and 90th percentile forecasts and applies conformal calibration to generate uncertainty intervals. This enables the system to estimate the probability that the reserve margin will fall below 10%, rather than providing only a single deterministic value. [1]

For scenario reasoning, the system uses unit-capacity information, outage scenarios, and domain rules to calculate the post-outage reserve capacity and reserve margin. The system also evaluates the supply-light level using both absolute reserve capacity and reserve-margin thresholds. [1]

The LLM is used only for intent understanding, task planning, and explanation. All numerical values are computed by deterministic tools and validated through a verification module. If a query exceeds the data coverage, such as nighttime peak load, private generation units, or unsupported future dates, the system safely refuses to answer rather than generating an unsupported response. [1]

## IV. System Design

The proposed system integrates five major data sources: Taipower daily supply-and-demand data, the past three years of daily peak reserve-margin data, weather observations from three stations, hydroelectric and thermal unit information, and official two-month forecasts. [1]

Data cleaning includes recomputing the reserve margin, correcting abnormal values, converting units, and cross-validating overlapping records from 2025. For example, erroneous reserve-margin values such as 202.0 are corrected to 20.2 through formula-based recalculation. [1]

The domain knowledge base includes supply-light rules, unit characteristics, day-and-night peak definitions, reserve-quality annotations, and the proposed “reserve divided by largest single-unit capacity” indicator. The largest single units are Taichung Units 1 and 2, each with a capacity of 1.3 GW. [1]

The system supports four query types: forecast, what-if scenario analysis, diagnosis, and explanation. For each query, the agent decomposes the task, invokes the appropriate tools, verifies the numerical output, and generates a traceable response with uncertainty and limitations. [1]

## V. Experimental Design

The evaluation uses 1,339 days of data from January 1, 2023 to August 31, 2026. The load-forecasting experiment applies rolling time-based splitting, with training data from January 2023 to August 2025 and testing data from September 2025 to August 2026. Baselines include monthly averages, same-weekday values from the previous week, and calendar-only models without weather features. [1]

The evaluation metrics include mean absolute error (MAE), mean absolute percentage error (MAPE), pinball loss, and 80% prediction-interval coverage. The scenario-analysis experiment uses the September 2025 Hsinta and Linkou outage events as retrospective cases, comparing scenarios with and without outage-capacity deduction. [1]

The agent evaluation includes approximately 40 questions covering forecasting, scenario analysis, attribution, explanation, and out-of-scope trap questions. The system is compared with a pure LLM, an LLM with retrieval-augmented generation (RAG), and the complete tool-constrained agent. Evaluation metrics include numerical accuracy, citation or traceability rate, correct-refusal rate, and hallucination rate. [1]

## VI. Expected Results

The weather and calendar features are expected to reduce load-forecasting error. Preliminary analysis shows that simple linear models achieve an average error of approximately 1.0–1.5 GW, substantially better than the 3.67 GW error obtained by predicting the historical mean. [1]

The scenario-reasoning module is expected to reproduce the direction and magnitude of the reserve-margin decline following the September 2025 Hsinta and Linkou outages. This would demonstrate that the system can function not only as a post-event explanation tool but also as a pre-event risk-awareness tool. [1]

The tool-constrained agent is expected to outperform a pure LLM in numerical accuracy and traceability. If the reserve margin itself remains difficult to predict using public data, the study will transparently report this limitation rather than overstating model accuracy. [1]

## VII. Contributions and Academic Value

### A. Main Contributions

1. **A Taiwan-specific reserve-risk reasoning system**  
   This study develops a domain AI agent that integrates load forecasting, unit-outage scenarios, low-reserve attribution, and supply-light interpretation using Taiwan’s public operational data. [1]

2. **A decision-oriented forecasting framework**  
   Instead of optimizing only a single forecasting-error metric, the system provides probabilistic load forecasts and links them to reserve-risk decisions. [1]

3. **A reusable power-grid knowledge base**  
   The study constructs a structured knowledge base containing supply-light rules, unit information, day-and-night peak definitions, reserve-quality labels, and N-1 risk indicators. [1]

4. **A data-quality correction workflow**  
   The study identifies and corrects erroneous reserve-margin records and establishes a reproducible validation process for public power-system data. [1]

5. **A quantifiable LLM reliability evaluation**  
   The study designs an evaluation set to measure numerical accuracy, traceability, refusal behavior, and hallucination rate in safety-critical power-system applications. [1]

### B. Academic Value

The academic value of this research lies in extending LLM-based grid agents from simulated-network analysis to real-world, publicly available operational data. Existing systems primarily focus on power-flow computation, contingency analysis, and violation repair, whereas this study addresses reserve adequacy, unit-outage scenarios, and operational decision support. [1]

The proposed tool-constrained architecture also responds to the reliability and explainability challenges of LLMs in power systems. By requiring all numerical outputs to originate from verified tools, the system reduces hallucination risk and improves user trust. [1]

Furthermore, the study connects probabilistic forecasting with operational decision-making. Rather than reporting only a single load forecast, the system provides uncertainty intervals and scenario-based reserve-risk assessments, making the results more useful for dispatch planning, policy analysis, and public communication. [1]

## VIII. Conclusion

This study proposes GridReserve Agent, a domain-specific AI system for Taiwan power-system reserve-risk assessment. The system combines weather-driven load forecasting, unit-outage scenario reasoning, low-reserve attribution, and supply-light interpretation in a traceable and verifiable framework.

The expected contribution is not merely a more accurate forecasting model, but a decision-support system that can explain why reserve risk changes, simulate the impact of unit outages, and identify the main contributors to low-reserve events. Through tool constraints, verification gates, and safe refusal mechanisms, the proposed system addresses the hallucination and traceability challenges of LLM-based power-system applications.

Future work may extend the system to nighttime peak reconstruction, renewable-generation uncertainty, demand-response scenarios, and real-time operational data integration.
